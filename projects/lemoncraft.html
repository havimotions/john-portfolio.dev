01 · THE PROBLEM

One community, too many disconnected systems.

Running LemonCraft meant managing a website, Discord community, player accounts, store purchases, reward delivery, leaderboards, support tickets, and in-game systems. The goal was to make those pieces feel like one platform instead of separate tools.

02 · THE SOLUTION

A web platform built around integration.

I worked on a platform where the website acts as the user-facing product layer, the Node.js backend handles web workflows and Discord functionality, and custom Minecraft integrations bridge live server data and rewards back into the experience.

WebResponsive store, profiles, leaderboards and dashboard features
DiscordOAuth, roles, tickets, announcements and community workflows
ServerRewards, player data, economy and plugin integration

03 · WHAT I BUILT

Product features across the whole stack.

  • Responsive store, cart, checkout, gifting, wallet and voucher flows.
  • Discord authentication, player profiles, leaderboards and account linking.
  • Daily Drops, personalized Lemon Market offers and Store Balance workflows.
  • Payment-proof upload and private Discord ticket checkout.
  • Admin tooling for products, categories, user-facing content and operations.
  • APIs and Java-side reward delivery through LemonBridge.

04 · ARCHITECTURE

The important part is how the pieces communicate.

The portfolio framing is intentionally broader than “a Minecraft website.” The useful engineering work is the same kind of work used in SaaS and internal platforms: authentication, APIs, state synchronization, commerce workflows, permissions, external integrations and production debugging.

05 · ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Reliability matters when separate systems share state.

Reward delivery

Store actions need to become game-side rewards without duplicate or lost delivery.

Identity linking

Web and Discord identities have to connect to the right player account.

Permissions

Staff, boosters, owners and regular users need different access across features.

Mobile UX

Store, profile and admin flows need to remain usable on smaller screens.

06 · WHAT I LEARNED

Building the feature is only half the job.

This project pushed me toward thinking in complete workflows: what happens before a user clicks, what services need to communicate afterward, what can fail, and what the user or staff member sees when it does.
